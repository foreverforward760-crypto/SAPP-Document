The Prism Analogy

A physical note on the prism analogy, because the physics matters: white light does not travel at c inside glass. The refractive index of glass is approximately 1.5, meaning light travels at roughly ⅔ of c inside the medium. Colors separate because different wavelengths slow by slightly different amounts — this is called dispersion. Shorter wavelengths (violet) slow more than longer ones (red), which is why they bend at different angles. Once light exits the prism, all wavelengths return to c in air. The prism doesn't reveal colors that were always traveling separately — it separates them by slowing them differentially inside a denser medium, then releases them. What the analogy preserves: the white beam was always composed of all wavelengths simultaneously. The prism doesn't create the colors — it reveals distinctions that were structurally present but undifferentiated in the beam. The mechanism of separation is slowing and bending through a denser medium — which maps, if anything, even more precisely to the SAPP: Stage 5 time-perception is the denser medium through which the one simultaneous event is slowed and separated into the apparent sequence of stages.
